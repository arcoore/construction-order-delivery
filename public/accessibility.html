SiteStock

Accessibility

Effective 6 September 2026 · Last updated 6 September 2026

We want SiteStock to be usable by everyone on a construction team, including people who use a keyboard only, a screen reader, screen magnification, or need larger text and higher contrast.

1. What we aim for

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA. SiteStock is a single evolving app rather than a set of fixed pages, and it has not yet had a full independent audit, so it does not fully meet that standard everywhere.

2. What currently works

  • Every screen can be reached and operated with the keyboard alone, and the focus outline is always visible.
  • A “Skip to main content” link is the first thing the keyboard reaches.
  • Text and interface colours have been checked for contrast against WCAG AA (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for controls and focus outlines).
  • Every form field has a label, and errors and confirmations are announced to screen readers.
  • Buttons and links are real buttons and links with clear text; decorative icons are hidden from screen readers.
  • The layout reflows to a single column on small screens and when text is enlarged, without loss of content.
  • The one type of image in the app - a driver’s delivery photo - carries alternative text.

3. Known limitations

  • The tabbed sections (for example the order tabs) are ordinary buttons. They work with Tab and Enter but do not yet support arrow-key movement or announce a “tab” role.
  • Pop-up dialogs move focus to the dialog and can be closed with Escape, but focus management has not been tested with every screen reader and browser combination.
  • Some status changes that happen live (for example a new order arriving) update on screen but are not always announced.
  • The app has not been tested with voice-control software.

We are working through these.

4. Tell us about a problem

If something stops you using SiteStock, or you need information in a different format, email arcooreacc@gmail.com with a short description of the problem, the screen you were on, and the assistive technology or browser you use. We aim to reply within 5 working days and will try to give you the information or function you need another way in the meantime.

5. Enforcement

In the UK, the Equality Act 2010 requires service providers to make reasonable adjustments for disabled people. If you contact us and are not satisfied with our response, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service (EASS).
